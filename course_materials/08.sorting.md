# Sorting: Branches, Networks and Vectors

Learning goals:
  * Understand `std::sort` as three techniques, not one algorithm
    * insertion sort at the leaves, quicksort's partition in the middle,
      heapsort as a safety net
    * what each one is for, and which one does the work on ordinary data
  * Measure what each technique is worth
    * the cutoff replaces recursion into small partitions with one cheap pass
    * the heapsort fallback never runs on random data — only on hostile
      inputs such as an organ pipe or an adversary
  * Recognize branch misprediction as the dominant cost of sorting random data
    * a branchy partition mispredicts about half its comparisons
    * a branch-free partition is why clang's `std::sort` is 3x faster than
      GCC's at a million elements
  * Know when a sorting network beats a comparison sort
    * fixed, data-independent comparisons: nothing to mispredict
    * mapping a network onto vector registers: permute, min/max, blend
  * Understand what SIMD can and cannot add to a partition
    * CompressStore, and why it is only a modest win on 4-lane NEON
